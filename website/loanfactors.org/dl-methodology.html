Methodology

How public fund filings become direct lending indices. Both indices follow one method for returns, exits, recoveries and weighting; they differ in their data sources. The full documentation and code are on GitHub.

OpenDL Europe

Data

Business development companies (BDCs) file their schedules of investments with the SEC in machine-readable form (XBRL), with the fair value, cost, principal and interest rate of every position. The SEC publishes these facts in its Financial Statement and Notes data sets; holding-level data are available from the third quarter of 2022 and cover all BDCs from 2023. The unit of the principal gives the loan currency.

Which loans are included

  • Loans in European currencies (EUR, GBP, CHF, SEK, NOK, DKK, PLN) held by BDCs whose business is direct lending; BDCs focused on real estate, infrastructure, asset-based or venture lending are excluded.
  • Cleaning: subtotals (by industry, country or asset class), cash, equity, revolvers, delayed-draw tranches, bonds, equipment finance and structured finance are removed. Mislabeled currencies are corrected: cost is reported in US dollars at historical rates and principal in the loan currency, so their ratio reveals the true currency.
  • Positions: identifiers are normalized (rates, spreads, dates, headings and affiliation notes removed), and positions renamed from one quarter to the next are linked by the distinctive words of the borrower name. About 85% of positions are found again in the following quarter.

Returns

  • Prices in loan currency: fair value converted with quarter-end Federal Reserve H.10 exchange rates, divided by principal.
  • Coupon: the tagged interest rate; else the rate stated in the identifier; else the 3-month base rate of the currency plus the stated spread; else the median of the BDC, currency and quarter.
  • Distress: BDCs do not tag non-accrual status. Loans described as non-accrual or valued below 70 earn no income; distressed exits are realized at a recovery of 70.8% of par, the estimate from OpenDL US.
  • Exits, cost basis and weighting as for OpenDL US below. Borrowers that the same BDC still holds in the next quarter under another identifier, tranche or currency are not counted as exits. The main index weights managers equally (at least ten loans per quarter).

Validation

  • Same loans in two sources: loans held by the same manager in a registered fund (Form N-PORT) and in a BDC are valued alike: median difference 0.18 points, 88% within one point. This confirms the processing of currencies, exchange rates and identifiers.
  • Index level: compared with the S&P Lincoln European Senior Debt Index, the first-lien sub-index has a correlation of 0.67 and differs by –0.80 percentage points per year; the value-weighted index of all loans differs by –1.7 points per year. Part of these differences reflects the measurement of income: the index uses contractual coupons, without fees and the accretion of discounts, which benchmarks built from income statements include. The rest reflects junior and unspecified loans and differences between the portfolios.
  • Sensitivity: the assumption that matters most is the treatment of exits valued between 80 and 95; treating them as repayments raises the index by up to 1.4 percentage points per year. This variant is published as an upper bound.

Limitations

  • The index covers European direct lending held by BDCs of US and global managers, not the whole European market.
  • Identifiers are free text; their normalization and linking are imperfect. Non-accrual status and payment-in-kind interest are not yet captured directly (payment-in-kind interest will be included from the next update).
  • Valuations, not transaction prices; gross of fees, unlevered.

OpenDL US

The construction, validation and properties of OpenDL US are documented in a working paper on SSRN.

Data

US registered investment funds report their complete holdings to the SEC in Form N-PORT, including the principal amount and fair value of every loan. Funds disclose these reports publicly for the last month of each fiscal quarter; most direct lending funds report at calendar quarter-ends. The index uses the quarterly data sets of the SEC from 2019 and identifies each fund's investment adviser and fund type from Form N-CEN.

Which loans are included

  • Funds: closed-end funds registered on Form N-2 (interval, tender offer and listed credit funds). Open-end mutual funds, whose private loans are mostly bespoke financings, are excluded.
  • Loans: corporate loans reported without a LoanX ID, CUSIP, ISIN or FIGI (placeholder identifiers count as none) and valued at fair value level 3, with at least USD 250,000 of principal.
  • Exclusions: unfunded or partially funded tranches that only pay a commitment fee, revolvers, delayed-draw tranches, broadly syndicated term loans B, real estate, asset-based, consumer and fund finance loans. Loans that stop paying interest (non-accrual) remain in the index.

Positions and returns

  • Positions: all tranches of a borrower in a fund are combined. Borrower names are normalized (legal forms, facility descriptions and dates removed) to follow loans from quarter to quarter.
  • Income: average coupon of the two quarter-ends divided by four and by the price at the start of the quarter; zero when the loan is in default or non-accrual. Payment-in-kind interest is included, because it increases the principal but not the price.
  • Price return: change in the fund's valuation per 100 of principal.
  • Exits: a loan that leaves a portfolio while the fund continues to report is treated as repaid at par if its last valuation was at least 95 and it was not in default. Loans that leave in default, in non-accrual or valued below 80 are realized at a recovery rate estimated from the data: the median valuation in the first quarter of non-accrual, 71% of par. Other exits are realized at the last valuation.
  • Components: for the value-weighted indices, the price return is split into realized losses (relative to an estimated cost basis), accretion of purchase discounts at repayment, and unrealized changes, as in the financial statements of business development companies.

Weighting

About three quarters of the market value is held by funds advised by Cliffwater. The main index therefore weights managers equally: within each manager, loans are weighted by market value; across managers with at least ten loans in the quarter, returns are averaged. A value-weighted index over all loans and a value-weighted index without Cliffwater are published alongside.

Validation

  • Index level: quarterly returns of the value-weighted index have a correlation of 0.93 with the Cliffwater Direct Lending Index (CDLI), interest income of 0.97. Calendar-year returns of the main index differ from the CDLI by 0.4 percentage points on average.
  • Credit indicators: without Cliffwater, the share of non-accrual loans and of loans valued below 90 are close to the levels reported for the CDLI.
  • Loan level: where several managers hold the same loan, their valuations of performing loans differ by about 0.7 points on average; valuations of stressed loans differ considerably more. Cliffwater does not value jointly held loans systematically higher than other managers.

Limitations

  • Returns are based on fund valuations, not transaction prices; they are smoothed and dampened relative to traded credit (Dimson beta on the leveraged loan market: 0.43).
  • Income excludes fees not reported in N-PORT, such as prepayment fees.
  • Business development companies, which hold most US direct loans, do not file Form N-PORT; the index covers registered funds only.
  • Returns are gross of fees and unlevered.
